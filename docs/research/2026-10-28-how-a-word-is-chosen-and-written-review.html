Map 3, Vocabulary - I89 2026-10-28, rounds 1-3 answered Design review, not a decision record

How a word is chosen and written

I89 How a word is chosen and written is the second ticket of map 3. It turns the rules in the map's Notes into decisions every later ticket (I90-I96) applies: when a single word may be a term and when it must be a compound, what a glossary entry carries, how the glossary opens and is grouped, and whether its growth is a risk to watch. It starts from I88's entries, the first written under the rule. Round 1 decides the rule itself and what it applies to. Rounds 2 and 3 (sections 5-6) decide the entry's shape, the opening and groups, and growth, which depend on round 1.

Answered

Round 1, Q1-Q4 as recommended (2026-10-28): a single word only where it matches, with the libraries named and D137's shared-core test (D170); every term passes the rule, and Origin, Manifest, Intent, Cursor, Engine and actor join I91 (D171); the compound every time in prose (D172); the map's rewrite and record rules, plus code comments (D173). Round 2 (section 5): Q5 labelled lines, with the note that the hover line should be formally the short, easy part, which led to the cut-off preview being found and Q5b; Q6-Q8 as recommended (D174-D177, D94'). Round 3: growth watched, not capped (D178). See I89's Answer.

The page in six lines

  1. vivief already did most of this, in one place: its quick reference opens with a one-line thesis, groups terms by context, names terms by their context (Code Diagnostics, UI Effect), keeps a table of replaced words with a reason each, and its reviews named "surface area growth" the biggest risk (section 1).
  2. The rule needs a test you can run: "matches the libraries we use" is open-ended until it names which libraries and what "matches" means. D137 One meaning of entity already gives the test for "matches": the two senses share their core, and the entry says the difference (section 2).
  3. Run on today's glossary, the rule passes most of its 67 single-word terms, finds the clashes I90-I92 already hold, and finds six no ticket holds: Origin, Manifest, Intent, Cursor, Engine and actor (section 2).
  4. A compound helps only if prose uses it too. The docs linker today keeps 15 words on an "everyday terms" list so a capitalised word links and a lower-case one does not; a compound written everywhere needs no such workaround (section 3).
  5. The map's other two rules (what a word change rewrites, where a word decision is written) worked as written in I88; one gap: code comments are prose too (section 4).
  6. The glossary grew from 81 to 123 terms (384 to 826 lines) in four weeks; 21 terms appear in two or fewer living documents. That is round 3's evidence for whether growth is a risk and how to measure it (section 6).

2. The rule, run on today's glossary

The charting's rule (the human, 2026-10-27)

A single-word term only where its meaning matches our own use and the libraries we use, inside and outside, as entity did (D137); otherwise a compound that names its context (a workflow runner, an OPFS worker, a test runner). Compounds are welcome where they make communication clearer. Effect's names come first (D135).

Two words in it need a fixed meaning before a ticket can apply it:

  • "The libraries we use." Proposed: Effect, checked fully (D135 Effect's names first); then the platforms and libraries our code imports or runs on, the ones a reader of our code meets: the web platform, Node.js, React Native and Expo, SQLite and Postgres, OpenTelemetry, TypeScript; and the vocabularies a decision borrows on purpose (Temporal's, D2; event sourcing's decide and evolve, D130). Not every meaning in software: "server" and "app" have dozens.
  • "Matches." Proposed, D137's test: the two senses share their core, and our entry says what differs. Entity passed it (Effect's Entity is an entity type that acts). If the cores differ, the word is a clash: a compound, or, where the other sense is far from anything our reader does, the bare word with a "not X's" mark.

Run with those two, on the 67 single-word terms of the 123 in GLOSSARY.md (the other 56 are compounds already). Clash readings for Effect are I78's and a check of its module names at 4.0.5; for the platforms they are mine, from general knowledge, to be confirmed in the ticket that takes the word.

What the sweep shows

The rule is cheap where it passes: 46 of 67 single words pass as they are, and 4 more need only a mark. It is not cheap where it fails: Origin and Intent are central words (ADR-0034 Origin trust, D167), and Intent is pinned nowhere but used in hundreds of places. Failing the test does not have to mean a rename: for each, the ticket that takes it weighs a compound ("content origin", "requested intent"?) against keeping the bare word with a mark ("Not the web's origin"). The rule only says the question must be asked and answered for every term, not what the answer is.

3. A compound in prose, three ways

If I91 decides "workflow runner" for our Worker (the charting's lean), here is one passage of a plan page written each way.

A. The compound always

"On web, the workflow runner resumes the run after a reload. The workflow runner reads its journal from the OPFS worker, which owns the database file."

Every use says which. "Worker" alone is only another project's sense (Effect's, the web's) and never links to our glossary.

B. Compound first, bare after

"On web, the workflow runner resumes the run after a reload. The runner reads its journal from the OPFS worker, which owns the database file."

Shorter. But "the runner" is also Effect's Cluster Runner, and an agent reading one paragraph from the middle, or a search hit, meets the bare word with no context.

C. Compound in the glossary only

"On web, the worker resumes the run after a reload. The worker reads its journal from the OPFS worker, which owns the database file."

Today's text. Two meanings of one word in one sentence.

What the linker does today

The docs site links the first use of a term on a page. For 15 terms that are also everyday words (EVERYDAY_TERMS in apps/docs/src/lib/references.ts: Accepted, Command, Deferred, Delivered, Effect, Execution, Feature, Frame, Layer, Origin, Qualified, Qualify, Service, Verify, Worker) it links only the capitalised form, not at a sentence start. That is a workaround for bare words: "the worker" in prose is not linked, "Worker" mid-sentence is, and a reader cannot tell which was meant. Under A, a term whose bare word clashes leaves that list, because the compound is never an everyday phrase. Under B and C the list stays and grows.

5a. The entry, two ways

Effect handler, as I88 wrote it (inline)

**Effect handler**:
A step that takes what is known and an effect asked
for, and decides which effects follow, without making
them happen. A command is one (...), authority's accept
another (...), and a workflow body a third ... Like
Elm's `update`, except that what it returns is proposed
and counts only once accepted. vivief's `effectHandler`,
in this form. Not an effect handler of algebraic effects
(OCaml 5, Koka), which resumes a paused computation;
Effect has no effect handler, and a handler alone keeps
Effect's meaning ...
_Avoid_: handler (alone, for this meaning)

The same, with labelled lines

**Effect handler**:
A step that takes what is known and an effect asked
for, and decides which effects follow, without making
them happen. A command is one (...), authority's accept
another (...), and a workflow body a third ...
vivief's `effectHandler`, in this form.
_Like_: Elm's `update`, except that what it returns is
proposed and counts only once accepted.
_Not_: an effect handler of algebraic effects (OCaml 5,
Koka), which resumes a paused computation; nor Effect's
handler, the code behind a declared operation.
_Avoid_: handler (alone, for this meaning)

What labelled lines buy: verify can check D162's "the except is required" (a _Like_ line without "except" fails), the docs site can show them apart from the definition, and I94's linker gets every other project's sense as a list it can read. The cost is a small change to the glossary parser (parseGlossary stops a definition at _Avoid_ today; it would stop at any label) and moving the marks already written inline (Effect handler, Command, Decision, Identity, Vision - View - Effects, Verified commit) into lines. A one-sentence mark ("Not GitHub's Verified badge") reads fine either way.
