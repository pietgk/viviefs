Grouping and order

Two kinds of connection on one map.

Parent, children and siblings describe how ideas are grouped. Previous and next describe a sequence. Follow the solid lines for grouping and the dashed arrows for order.

Solid lines = grouping. Dashed arrows = sequence. Siblings connect through a shared parent.

A sibling is a peer.

“Cook pasta” and “Make salad” both belong to “Make dinner”. Neither contains the other. Sharing that parent makes them siblings.

Order is a separate connection.

Previous and next mean before and after in a chosen sequence. They do not mean the notes you last visited. A node can be both a sibling and a next step.

The middle is your viewpoint.

In K-Plex, clicking a node makes it the new center. The relationships are then shown from that node’s point of view. The underlying connections stay the same.

For engineers: the same idea in graph terms
Make dinner → child → Cook pasta
Cook pasta → child → Boil water
Cook pasta → child → Add pasta
Cook pasta → child → Drain pasta

Gather ingredients → next → Cook pasta
Cook pasta → next → Serve dinner

Make dinner → child → Make salad
Make dinner → child → Set table

Parent and child are inverse views of one relationship. If A is a parent of B, B is a child of A. Previous and next are inverse views too.

Siblings are derived: two distinct nodes share at least one parent. They do not need a separate sibling link. A node may have multiple parents, so the structure can be a graph rather than a single tree.

These examples use parent/child to mean “part of”. For other notes it can mean “a kind of” or another defined grouping. Give the relationship a clear meaning; screen position alone cannot define it.
