Round 1: the destination and the rules of the map

Q1 - What is the destination?

I34: "a glossary where each term means one thing". Map 2 made its version checkable: every row disposed of in a ticket's Answer, and one ADR the human accepted. Map 3's thing to accept is the glossary itself.

Q2 - Does map 3 change the words, or only decide them?

Map 2 was "plan, don't do": decisions, glossary entries, and an issue for every consequence. For words that leaves the glossary saying runner while ADR-0036 says worker, which is the two meanings the map exists to remove. The linker (I26) is code in apps/docs.

Q3 - When a word changes, what is rewritten and what is kept?

Today's rules: attributes, pinned names and the RPC are never renamed (D44, D44'); research records are kept as written (D68); a decision whose meaning changes gets a primed row (D14'). A word-only change is new. The living documents are the ones pnpm refs fix covers: the glossary, AGENTS.md, ADRs, issues, plan pages and guides.

Q4 - Where is a word decision written down?

The glossary holds the term; domain modeling says the glossary is not a place for decisions. Many of map 3's answers are small marks ("not Effect's Envelope") with nothing weighed; some are real choices (runner or worker, what Effects is called).

Q5 - What is the map called?

I34 calls it Vocabulary; the sequence and the handover say "map 3, the words". Both are plain. The effort slug becomes docs/plan/<slug>/ and every ticket's Effort: line.

Q6 - Which inputs are map 3's?

Clusters 1-6 above are clearly map 3's. Two edges: cluster 7 (attribute tense and a namespace per consumer app) is about names a consumer app will pin, and I32 (explain any word on demand) is a reading feature whose trigger is I26.

Q7 - The effect description: is this the direction to start from?

A draft, written the way D137 settled entity, for the first ticket's review page to test on real code. Effect: "Something that happens and makes a difference: a fact written, a message sent, a screen redrawn, a model asked." Then: "Effect, the TypeScript library, writes an effect down as a value of its type Effect and runs it later; that describing and running are two steps is how the library works, not another meaning." Effect handler: "A step that takes what is known and an effect that comes in, and decides which effects follow." A command is the data level's effect handler (a read model and an intent in, a changeset or a typed error out), and the engine runs a workflow's steps the same way. Open for the ticket: whether an intent is an effect asked for and not yet accepted, and what the formula's parts are called (I30).

Q9 - What "keeping an old word does not get in the way" checks

My reading of your Q3 note. Before a ticket retires a word, it checks three things and writes the result in its Answer: (1) no persisted name or code identifier uses the word in the retired sense, or an issue is opened for it; (2) no planned concept (open issues, maps 4-6, the Schedule) needs that bare word for another meaning; (3) if a retired word is ever given a new meaning later, its entry says what old records meant by it.

Q10 - The map's name, with the reasons

I offered only the two names already in use (I34's and the sequence's). Your two, and what each says:

  • "The glossary": names the artifact the close accepts, but the map does more than GLOSSARY.md: prose across living documents, the site's linker, attribute names (cluster 7).
  • "Terms": a term is a word with a glossary entry ("glossary term" in 22 files, the linker's termFinder). The map also decides words that are deliberately not our terms (I26) and attribute names, which are not terms.
  • "Vocabulary": the glossary already says it "fixes the vocabulary the stack uses", AGENTS.md lists it as "vocabulary", and P05's "vocabulary pass" is about attribute names. It covers terms, non-terms and names, which with Q6 B is all seven clusters.
  • "The words": plain, but says least as a folder name.
