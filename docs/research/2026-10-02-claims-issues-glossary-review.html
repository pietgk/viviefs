Claims, issues as records, and a glossary a newcomer can read

Three proposals from your round-3 review: call the numbered things claims, keep open issues as files the docs can reference, and link every glossary term so a reader who stumbles gets a plain answer on hover. Each section says where I agree, where I push back, and what I would build. Decision names (D88): recorded as agreed.

1. Claims or gates, and whether P04 becomes C04

You are right that the reader meets claims: "the log store contract holds on every store" is what matters, and the sidebar calls the chapter Evidence. But "claim" and "gate" are two different things, and the docs read better when both words stay, each with one meaning:

Renaming P04 to C04: I recommend against it

  • The ledger holds 168 runs recorded under P01 to P11. It is append-only and machine-written: renaming means either rewriting history we promised never to touch, or keeping a P-to-C map forever.
  • 80 tracked files carry the id in their name (probes/p04.ts, 2026-09-20-p04.md), plus an environment variable the evidence app reads, and hundreds of references.
  • The gain is one letter. With "P is for proof", P04 reads as "proof 04": the proof of one claim. That is your intent, without the rename.

What I would build instead: put the claim in front

  • Claim becomes a glossary term: "a statement the stack depends on that could be false; each gate proves exactly one". Gate: "the check that proves one claim; P is for proof".
  • gates.ts gets a claim field: the statement, taken from the plan's "Fact to establish" column. The gate's short name stays.
  • A generated Reference > Claims page: one row per gate, with id, claim, pass condition, positive control, the ADRs that depend on it and every evidence note. The Evidence chapter becomes Claims and evidence.
  • P04 anywhere links to its row on that page, and the hover shows the claim.

2. Open issues as files the docs can reference

Agreed, and the vendored skills already fit it: triage, to-tickets and wayfinder work against "the issue tracker", which docs/agents/issue-tracker.md already defines as committed Markdown with the triage roles as a Status: line. What is missing is one place with stable ids. Today open items live in four places: the deferred-decisions table, the risks table, handover notes, and upstream-issues.md.

## Comments
  • One directory, docs/issues/NNNN-slug.md, numbered like ADRs and never reused. Tickets that an effort's spec breaks into go there too, with an Effort: line, so every issue has one global id. The issue-tracker.md adaptation changes; the vendored skills do not.
  • States are the triage roles (needs-triage, needs-info, ready-for-agent, ready-for-human, wontfix) plus resolved. A resolved issue keeps its file, like a superseded ADR.
  • Reference > Issues on the site, with a status badge per issue, like the ADRs. I1 anywhere renders as "I1 node-forge advisory fails audit", linked, with the first line of "What" on hover. An unknown id fails the build.
  • Migrated: the 14 deferred decisions, the open drift, the P07 notification tap, the effect-solutions peer warning, the API reference question, accepting the log-store guide, and what P stands for, which this page answers. Risks stay a table; each risk can link its issue.

The id: why not #1

The skills speak of #42, but the docs already use #6347 for an Effect issue on GitHub. A bare #N would be ours on one page and upstream's on the next, and the build could not tell them apart. I42 follows P04 and D37; issue-tracker.md tells the skills that "#42" said in conversation means I42.

3. A glossary a newcomer can read, linked from every page

Agreed on the mechanism: the plugin and the preview card from round 2 can link glossary terms exactly as they link decisions. But your reader test shows the deeper problem is the definitions, not the links. A hover that shows "a reusable architectural solution... green in its exemplar" makes the stumble faster, not shorter. So two parts, and the second matters more.

a. Definitions a newcomer can read

Every definition starts with one plain sentence that someone outside software understands, and the hover shows that sentence. The precise part follows, for people and agents who need it, and may use other glossary terms, which are linked.

Pattern, today

A reusable architectural solution with one contract and possibly several implementations (log store, telemetry sink, identity, encrypted store). Delivered only when qualified with evidence, green in its exemplar, and its guide accepted.

Pattern, proposed

A proven way to build one part of an app, written down once so every app here builds that part the same way. It has one contract (what it promises) and may have several implementations (the log store works on SQLite and on Postgres). It counts as delivered when gates have proven its claims, its exemplar passes verify, and a person has accepted its guide.

Missing terms get entries, because the docs lean on them: claim, evidence note, ADR, decision, contract, qualified, accepted, delivered, Effect, service, layer. "Green" leaves the docs: it becomes "passes verify". I draft every first sentence; you test them with the same reader; what still confuses him gets rewritten.

Where I push back: linking every term on every page by machine is reliable for ids like D37, which mean one thing, and less so for words, which mean several. First use per page and a short everyday-words list keep the links right. The real test is your reader: after the rewrite, does he understand "pattern" from its hover?
