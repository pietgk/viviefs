Round 1: the destination

Q1 - What is the destination?

Wayfinder plans by default: tickets resolve decisions, nobody does the work. An inventory is different: the record is the deliverable, and maps 2-6 are where the decisions are made. So the map's Notes would override "plan, don't do": research tickets read their slice and write it into the record.

Q2 - Which sources are in scope?

The table above, as choices. The defaults are my recommendation.

Q3 - What is one row?

A file-per-row inventory is easy to check for completeness but buries the ideas: concepts v6 alone holds dozens. An idea-per-row inventory is what maps 2-6 need, but nothing proves every file was read.

Q4 - How does a row say where an idea stands?

I34 names four statuses: taken, different, missing, rejected and why. The rows above use three columns instead of one.

Statustaken (same idea), different (same word or area, another meaning or design), deferred (a slot, hypothesis or Decide when: exists), missing (nothing in ViViEfs), rejected (a decision rules it out). Deferred and rejected need a ViViEfs citation; without one the idea is missing.
LevelFor taken and different, how far ViViEfs has got (the working agreement keeps these apart): accepted (a D-number or glossary term), proposed (an ADR, unverified), implemented, verified (the gate that passed).
Scopereference stack (a candidate for ViViEfs), product vision (counseling, procurement, a product's peer-to-peer), DevAC tooling (code analysis, LikeC4, browser automation). Only reference-stack rows get a map and a question.

Q5 - Does the inventory also look the other way?

I34 asks "what did ViViEfs learn that vivief lacked": gates and the ledger, claims, verify and its evidence registry, delivery by guide, exercise and lesson. Map 2 needs that answer as much as the forward one.

Round 2: the frontier

Your note on Q5 changes the shape. An inventory that is complete in both directions is no longer a vivief table with an appendix; it is one table of ideas that each have two sides, and coverage on both sides. That makes ViViEfs's own ideas something to enumerate up front, not something to sweep at the end.

Q6 - One table with two sides?

Every row is one idea with a vivief side and a ViViEfs side; either may be empty. A ViViEfs-only row gets the status ViViEfs only and names its nearest vivief idea. Coverage then has two lists: every in-scope vivief file, and every ViViEfs source: the 101 glossary terms, the decisions D1-D94, ADR-0001 to ADR-0031, the gates P01-P17 and the log-store guide. Q7 - How the reading splits into tickets

A ticket must fit one agent session of about 100K tokens. vivief's in-scope docs are about 44,000 lines, so the reading needs about eight slices, cut along vivief's own seams. Two index tickets come first and run side by side: one names vivief's ideas from its backbone, the other names ViViEfs's ideas from its sources. Every slice then matches against the same two indexes, so eight agents don't produce eight names for one idea.

Q8 - Where each ticket writes

Slices running side by side cannot all edit one record without conflicts, and a research record is written once and then kept as it was (D68).

Q9 - How the research tickets run

Wayfinder starts a research subagent per research ticket when the map is charted. Only I35 and I36 are unblocked then. The eight slices could run as a swarm once both close; your standing rule is that a swarm needs your approval first, so here are the trade-offs.

Swarm of 8 subagentsOne session per ticket
TimeOne sittingEight sittings, or several sessions you start in parallel
TokensAbout the same in total, about 8 x 100K, spent at onceThe same, spread out
ConsistencyAgents can't see each other; the shared indexes and I45's checks carry itEach later session can read the rows already written
Your attentionOnce, at I46You can glance at each slice as it lands

Standing rules for the map's Notes (no question, tell me if one is wrong)

  • Every row cites file and section heading on both sides, so a reader can check it in one click.
  • verified names the gate and only stands if the ledger records a pass; rejected and deferred only stand with a ViViEfs citation. I45 rechecks all three.
  • Rows record and ask; they never decide. A recommendation belongs to the map that owns the question (2-6).
  • Names follow GLOSSARY.md on the ViViEfs side and vivief's own words on the vivief side, in code font, so a reader always knows whose word it is.

Fog and out of scope, as I'd write them on the map

  • Not yet specified: how the HTML view is laid out (grouped by concept, by map, or by status), decided when the rows exist.
  • Out of scope: deciding what ViViEfs adopts (maps 2-6); vivief's archive and code; vivief's GitHub issues.
