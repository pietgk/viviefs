A. Guaranteed work while the app is closed not yet at all

Code running on a guaranteed schedule while the app is closed. The OS only guarantees local notifications; background tasks are opportunistic (D3, D24).

Since then: nothing changed the reason. iOS still guarantees only a local notification. What D14' added is that the holder of a run is derived from its journal, so due work never lives in memory: a wake_at datom and the run's head say what is due and who wrote last.

Proposed: Code running on a guaranteed schedule while the app is closed. The OS only guarantees local notifications; background tasks are opportunistic (D3, D24). Must stay possible: due work is a fact in the log, never a timer in memory (a wake_at datom, the run's journal), and resuming needs only the journal, so a later OS guarantee or a background task can pick it up by the same sweep (D24, D14'). Not yet at all: the limit is the platform's; a lab cannot lift it.

Worked example: a reminder is due at 09:00 while the phone is in a drawer. Today the notification fires, the person taps it, the app launches and sweeps due clocks. If iOS one day guarantees a background run, the same sweep runs there: nothing in the engine waits on a process that was alive.

B. Two writers on one workflow execution, merged afterwards not yet at all two items become one

Several people editing the same workflow execution at once (rejected in D13).

Multi-writer merge of workflow executions (D14): one lease holder at a time.

Since then: D14' replaced the lease with the journal fence: one writer at a time, a writer that is behind is refused, and a run is always one history. "Rejected" is the word D100 rejects. And the two lines are one thing seen twice: several people at once on one execution is several writers whose histories would need merging. What several people already do together is feed one run through its outside inputs: a reviewer approves on the web while the phone holds the run (D11, D40), write-once on their own entity, not moving the head.

Proposed: Two writers on one workflow execution at once, their histories merged afterwards. A run has one writer at a time and is always one history; a writer that is behind is refused (D14'), because an activity's side effects cannot be merged afterwards. Must stay possible: several people, on any device or the server, work on one run through its outside inputs, write-once on their own entity, which never move the head (D11, D40); a refused history strands no user content (D147, D148). Not yet at all: a design choice, not a production gap; no app has asked.

Worked example: the field worker's phone holds the evidence run; the reviewer approves on the web at the same time. Not two writers: the approval completes a deferred, the phone reads it on its next step. Two writers would be the phone and a tablet both uploading the photo step offline; one is refused, and its photo, a domain changeset, is still kept (D147).

C. Production hosting, deployment and telemetry learnable in the lab

Production hosting, deployment topology and production telemetry backends.

Since then: D101 made the lab a place to learn production shapes. Part of this already runs in the lab: Keycloak (P11) and Jaeger and otel-lgtm (P10) as digest-pinned Apple Container images. I7 holds the open questions (where the server runs, TLS, which telemetry backend, the web app's sign-in session) with Decide when: the first consumer app leaves the lab.

Proposed: Production hosting, deployment topology and production telemetry backends (I7). Must stay possible: the server is a composition of Layers configured from its environment, telemetry leaves through OTLP to any backend (the P10 sinks are the lab's), and identity is any OIDC provider behind the Identity service (D47). Production only, learnable in the lab (a local Kubernetes with the server, Postgres, Keycloak and an OTLP backend). I7's Decide when: gains "or the human schedules it for learning in the lab", as I8 and I60 have.

D. Several servers, organizations spread across them learnable in the lab new on the list

Why add it: D101 names it, with engine scale-out, as the two items learnable in the lab, and I59 asks the list to say so; but the list has no such line. I58 holds it, Decide when: a consumer app needs organizations on more than one server, or you schedule it.

Proposed: Several servers, with organizations spread across them (I58). Today one server holds authority for every organization (D103). Must stay possible: authority is held per organization, so which node holds an organization's authority can differ between organizations; every request and changeset names its organization and the server never trusts a client's (D18); tx is corrected against the node holding authority, not "the server" (D118-D123). Production only, learnable in the lab.

Not the same as F: here each organization still has one authority, only not all on the same server. F moves or shares an organization's authority.

E. Engine scale-out learnable in the lab names a dropped path

Horizontal scale-out of the workflow engine (Effect Cluster is the later path, D39).

Since then: D39' One engine, one journal, one fence: scale-out is more runners writing on the journal head; Cluster is not the path (it fences by time only, keeps a second journal, assumes always-reachable runners), and stays possible behind WorkflowEngine if a measured load needs it.

Proposed: Horizontal scale-out of the server's workflow engine: more runners writing on each run's journal head (D39', I8). Must stay possible: the head check is made in one step with the append (D14'), a runner claims a step before acting, and WorkflowEngine stays the one interface, so Cluster stays possible behind it. Production only, learnable in the lab.

F. Server-independent mode learnable in the lab already in D100's form

A server-independent mode: a group keeps working without its original operator, with authority moved to another node or shared by all (D104, I60). Must stay possible: [D105's seven]. Learnable in the lab (D101).

Proposed: unchanged. I54 made it easier (a hash chain makes forwarded history checkable; signed and bound elsewhere is its proof of authorship, D156), and no map-2 decision made a D105 item harder. The ADRs' Trade-offs already say so one by one.

G. Product-specific choices of the consumer apps a rule, not a "not yet"

Product-specific decisions of the consumer apps (for example BirVana's XState and voice ADRs).

Since then: the consumer apps live here (D55'), each a <product>-client + <product>-server with its own features and attribute namespace (D106). So product choices will be made in this repository; what stays out is the stack taking them on. That is not "out for now, never ruled out": it is who owns what, and it should never stop being true. Under D100 every line of the list should one day be possible; this one should not.

Proposed: remove it from Out of scope and add to In scope: "Each consumer app makes its own product-specific choices (BirVana's XState and voice ADRs, for example) in its own apps, features and attribute namespace (D106); the stack's ADRs and guides take none of them (D55')." Where a consumer app keeps its own ADRs is decided with the first one.

The alternative, keeping it in the list as "not yet at all", reads as if the stack might one day adopt BirVana's voice ADRs, which is the coupling D55 and D55' both avoid.

Not proposed for the list

Map 2 deferred several features, each an issue with a Decide when:: live co-editing of text (I83), agent accounts (I86), signed changesets (D156, I15), group and role encryption levels (I87), a server copy of quarantine (I11). Proposal: they stay in their issues and the Schedule, not on the list. The list is for scope that shapes the architecture, where a decision now could close a door; each of these has its door already named in its decision (the envelope's room for a signature, the conflict policy per attribute, and so on).
