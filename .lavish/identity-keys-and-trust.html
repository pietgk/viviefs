Identity, keys and trust

What "signed in" actually means, who vouches for what, how a device labels its own writes, and how our account-based model compares with vivief's key-based model and with Holochain. The page is meant for reasoning now and for the deep dive later.

The whole answer in four sentences
  1. Signing in means a provider account proved itself to the identity provider, and the device now holds tokens that prove it. It is not "the device" and not "the person" signing in.
  2. The person is our server's concept. The server maps provider accounts to people, and only the server can.
  3. A device never decides who it is. It keeps a copy of the server's statement (the Caller RPC) and labels its writes with it; the server checks the label again on every write.
  4. A key-based model (vivief, Holochain) would make identity cryptographic and checkable by anyone. That belongs in one deep-dive step, together with tamper-evidence, before the first app that makes audit claims.

2. What signing in means

A precise version of "the device is signed in": the holder of a provider account authenticated at the identity provider, and the identity provider gave the device tokens that say so. The device carries the proof; it did not authenticate, and it does not become anybody.

sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  participant H as Alice (holder)
  participant D as Device
  participant K as Identity provider
  participant S as Our server
  D->>K: start sign-in (PKCE)
  H->>K: password or passkey (authentication)
  K-->>D: tokens for provider account (iss, sub)
  Note over D: sign-in session: signed in
  D->>S: call + access token
  S->>S: verify token, look up person
  S-->>D: answer as Caller (person P7)
  
stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> SignedIn: sign in (PKCE)
  SignedIn --> SignInNeeded: refresh refused (invalid_grant)
  SignInNeeded --> SignedIn: sign in again
  SignedIn --> [*]: sign out (session ends, local replica closes)
  SignInNeeded --> [*]: sign out

"Signed out" is not a state of a session: signing out ends the session. The local replica stays on the device until someone removes the account from it (Q11).

3. How a device labels its writes (Q22)

Every changeset carries envelope.actor, and the server refuses it unless that is the caller's person. The device has to write that label before it reaches the server, because offline drafts exist. So it keeps a copy of the server's own statement.

sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  participant D as Device (sign-in session)
  participant S as Our server
  D->>S: Caller
  S-->>D: person P7, memberships [acme]
  Note over D: kept inside the sign-in session
  D->>D: offline edit, envelope.actor = P7
  D->>S: Append (actor P7)
  S->>S: actor == caller's person? member of acme?
  S-->>D: AppendAck
  Note over D,S: on a refusal (MembershipMissing, ActorMismatch) the copy is stale: fetch Caller again

What the device knows

Nothing about itself that anyone trusts. It holds tokens (the identity provider's statement) and a copy of Caller (the server's statement). Both expire or go stale.

What the server trusts

Only the token's signature and its own log. The label on a changeset is a claim the server checks, every time.

A token is not a certificate. The identity provider signs tokens with a key, and the server trusts that key because it fetched it from the provider's own URL. What chains to a root authority is the TLS certificate of that URL, which is why production needs TLS.

5. Three models side by side

The biggest difference is where identity is rooted: an account at a provider, or a key the device holds.

What vivief already concluded

  • Holochain's split between validation rules every peer runs and application logic validated the Contract / effectHandler split; the runtime was not adopted: "Vivief is a small trusted-party system. Massive overhead."
  • Identity is datoms: external ids, keypairs and device links on the same entity.
  • Transport identity and application authorization are separate concerns.

6. Known limitations, recorded not fixed (Q23)

The device id is not authenticated

envelope.device is whatever the device writes. Nothing binds it to a key or to a person. A key per device, linked to its person, would.

Who may take over a lease is unspecified

Any member of an organization can take over any execution's lease there by raising the epoch. That is how failover works (D15), but which member, and which device, is an authorization question for roles and device identity.

Both are in ADR-0022. P11 still enforces what its gate claims: membership on sync, lease and commands.

8. Check yourself

Alice signs in on her phone. Who authenticated?

Alice, as the holder of a provider account, at the identity provider. The phone only started the flow and received the tokens.

Does Keycloak know that Alice is person P7?

No. Keycloak knows provider accounts. Only our server maps a provider account to a person, through the account entity in its log.

A tampered phone writes actor = P9. What happens?

Locally, nothing stops it. When it pushes, the server compares the label with the caller's person and answers ActorMismatch. The label is a claim, not an identity.

Why not fix the device id in P11?

Doing it properly means device keys, and that is the same decision as signed changesets. Deciding half of it now would mean deciding it twice.
